## Chapter 8: Domain 4: Responsible AI—Bias, Fairness & SageMaker Clarify — Flashcards

| # | Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
|---|-----------------|---------------|
| 1 | SageMaker Clarify: What's the difference between pre-training and post-training bias reports? | PRE-TRAINING: analyzes training DATA before model training (class imbalance, demographic representation). POST-TRAINING: analyzes deployed MODEL predictions (disparate impact, accuracy differences between groups). Exam tip: 'discovered after deployment' = post-training bias. |
| 2 | What threshold indicates problematic class imbalance in training data? | Majority class >80% = risk of over-predicting that class. Example: 92% approved loans → model over-predicts 'approved'. Exam tip: any percentage over 80% in a scenario = likely testing imbalance awareness. |
| 3 | Disparate Impact (DI) formula and threshold for bias? | DI = (favorable outcome rate group A) / (favorable outcome rate group B). DI < 0.8 indicates significant bias. Example: 76% accuracy for <25 age / 94% overall = 0.809 DI (borderline). Exam heavily tests this calculation. |
| 4 | What do SHAP values measure in SageMaker Clarify? | SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) = contribution of each feature to individual predictions. Positive SHAP = increases prediction, negative = decreases. Used for model explainability and identifying bias-causing features. Exam tip: SHAP = explainability, not bias detection itself. |
| 5 | Bedrock Guardrails: What are the 3 main configuration types? | 1) CONTENT FILTERS: hate/violence/sexual/insults (thresholds: none/low/medium/high). 2) PII REDACTION: SSN, credit cards, names, addresses. 3) DENIED TOPICS: block specific discussion topics. Exam tests knowing which feature for which requirement. |
| 6 | What must a Model Card include for responsible AI transparency? | 5 mandatory elements: 1) MODEL PURPOSE, 2) PERFORMANCE METRICS, 3) KNOWN LIMITATIONS, 4) INTENDED USE CASES, 5) INAPPROPRIATE USE CASES explicitly stated. Exam tip: documents WHAT and WHY, not HOW (technical details optional). |
| 7 | Bedrock content filter thresholds: How do LOW vs HIGH differ? | LOW = blocks only most egregious content (permissive). MEDIUM = balanced. HIGH = blocks more content (strict). Thresholds apply to: violence, hate, sexual, insults, misconduct, prompt attacks. Exam tip: HIGH ≠ context-aware, just more sensitive. |
| 8 | What types of PII can Bedrock Guardrails redact? | SSN, credit card numbers, names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, bank account numbers, driver's license numbers. Exam tip: PII redaction = sensitive data, NOT offensive content (use content filters for that). |
| 9 | When should you use SageMaker Clarify vs Bedrock Guardrails? | SageMaker Clarify = ML model BIAS DETECTION and EXPLAINABILITY (pre/post-training bias, SHAP). Bedrock Guardrails = LLM CONTENT FILTERING (hate/PII/denied topics). Exam trap: using Clarify for LLM content filtering or Guardrails for ML bias. |
| 10 | What is 'hallucination' in LLMs and what's the legal risk? | Hallucination = model generates false/fabricated information confidently. Legal risks: medical misinformation (liability), financial advice (regulatory), IP infringement (training data copyright). Exam tip: mitigation = denied topics + human review + disclaimers, NOT more training data. |
| 11 | Human-in-the-loop: When is it required for responsible AI? | Required for HIGH-RISK outputs: medical advice, legal decisions, financial approvals, content moderation. Human reviews AI decision before action. Exam tip: combine with technical controls (Guardrails) + documentation (Model Card) for complete mitigation. |
| 12 | DPPL (Difference in Positive Proportions in Predicted Labels) vs DI (Disparate Impact)? | DPPL = difference in PREDICTION RATES between groups (what model predicts). DI = ratio of FAVORABLE OUTCOME RATES (correctness/approval). Example: DPPL = model predicts 'buy' for 60% of group A, 80% of group B. DI = 76% accuracy for A / 94% for B. Exam differentiates these. |
